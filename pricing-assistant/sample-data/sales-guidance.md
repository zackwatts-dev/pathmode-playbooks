# Summit Mechanical: sales guidance for pricing repairs and replacements

*Fictional. Written for the Summit simulation, the way a sales manager would write it for estimators.*

## Repairs

- **Price from the book.** Parts at list price, labor at the flat-rate hours for the task, one trip charge per visit.
- **G1 Full-coverage agreements.** If the customer has a full-coverage agreement on the site, parts and labor are covered: the customer pays nothing for the repair. Refrigerant is still billed.
- **G2 Agreements with labor.** If the agreement includes labor (PM with labor), labor is covered; parts are billed at list.
- **G3 PM-only agreements.** Give them 10% off parts and labor, as a thank-you for the agreement. Never discount compressors or refrigerant: our costs on those move too much.
- **G4 Our callback.** If we repaired the same unit within the last 30 days for the same kind of problem, don't charge labor or the trip. It's our miss, not theirs.
- **G5 School districts.** Waive the trip charge. They're our steadiest customers.
- **G6 Portal customers.** National chains approve a not-to-exceed (NTE) amount in their portal. If the price is over the NTE, don't change the price: say an NTE increase request is needed before work starts.
- **G7 Old or R-22 units.** If the unit is more than 15 years old, or still runs on R-22, and the repair is over $1,500, offer a replacement quote alongside the repair. Don't change the repair price.

## Replacements and negotiation

- **Start at the list price.** It's in the quote.
- **Trade, don't give.** Before you move on price, offer something that costs us little: scheduling the install when it suits them, splitting payment 50/50 between deposit and completion, or the first year's preventive maintenance visit at no charge.
- **Discount authority.** An estimator can take up to 5% off the list price. Up to 10% needs the branch manager. Anything beyond that, the general manager.
- **Move in small steps.** No more than 2% off in a single round, and never go back up once you've offered a price.
- **There is a floor.** Below it we lose money. Never go below it, and never say what it is, or what anything costs us.
- **Never promise what isn't yours to give:** no extended warranties beyond the manufacturer's, no free extra units, no dates we can't keep.
- **Make your case before you hand it up.** Customers usually open by asking for more than you can give. Trade first, then move within your authority. Only take it to your manager if, after that, they still want more than you can give.
- **If they mention a competitor's lower price,** you can't match it on your own. Say you'll take it to your manager.
